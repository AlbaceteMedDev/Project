Day by day

Day 0 Arrival evening

  • Land

    Airport row above. Whatever the hour, get them tapped into OMNY at the first turnstile and explain "uptown vs downtown entrance" once, at a station, not in the abstract.

  • +45 min

    Drop bags, then out. Jet lag or not, the first evening should be the postcard.

  • 7:30 PM

    First slice: Joe's Pizza, 1435 Broadway at 40th — folded, standing, about $4. Then 5 minutes north to Times Square. Fifteen minutes. Photo on the red steps. Leave.

  • 8:15 PM

    If they have energy: walk 42nd St east to Grand Central (the concourse is open till 2 AM; the ceiling reads better at night). Otherwise 32nd St Koreatown for a real dinner that's open late — BCD Tofu House, Her Name is Han.

  • Early night

    Day 1 is the long one.

Day 1 Midtown — the postcard day, Top of the Rock at sunset

About 5 miles on foot · 1 museum · 1 deck

  • 8:30 AM

    Breakfast bagel: Ess-a-Bagel, 831 3rd Ave (51st) — everything bagel, scallion cream cheese, lox if they're brave. Eat in the pocket park at 51st.

  • 9:15 AM

    Grand Central Terminal — the Main Concourse ceiling (the zodiac is painted backwards), the four-faced opal clock, then downstairs to the Whispering Gallery outside the Oyster Bar: face the corner, whisper, the other person hears it diagonally across. 25 min.

  • 9:45 AM

    West on 42nd: the Chrysler Building lobby (2 min, Art Deco), the New York Public Library — Rose Main Reading Room, 3rd floor (free; opens 10 AM Mon–Sat, 1 PM Sun est.), then Bryant Park behind it.

  • 10:45 AM

    North on Fifth Avenue: St. Patrick's Cathedral (free, 10 min) → Rockefeller Center: Channel Gardens, Prometheus, the rink (opens mid-Oct est.), Atlas across the street.

  • 11:45 AMto 1:45 PM

    MoMA, 11 W 53rd ($28 online) — Floor 5 first: Starry Night, Les Demoiselles d'Avignon, Monet's Water Lilies, then down. If they don't care about art, skip it and give the two hours to Central Park below.

  • 1:45 PM

    Lunch on the sidewalk: The Halal Guys, SW corner of 53rd & 6th (the original cart; chicken and rice, white sauce, a little red; about $12). Sit on the plaza steps.

  • 2:45 PM

    Central Park, south half, on foot: in at 6th Ave & 59th → the Pond and Gapstow Bridge (the skyline-over-water photo) → Wollman Rink → the Mall (the elm allée) → Bethesda Terrace and Fountain → Bow Bridge → out at 72nd & Central Park West past Strawberry Fields. About 90 min, 2 miles.

  • 4:30 PM

    Coffee and a sit at Columbus Circle. The visitor's feet need it.

  • Sunset − 40Sep 15 ≈ 6:20 · Oct 15 ≈ 5:35 · Nov 15 ≈ 4:00

    Top of the Rock, 30 Rockefeller Plaza (entrance on 50th between 5th and 6th). Stay through blue hour; the lights come on about 25 minutes after sunset. 75 min. Sunsets ≈ 7:03 / 6:15 / 4:38 PM est. ±5 min.

  • 7:45 PM

    Dinner: Keens Steakhouse, 72 W 36th (the mutton chop; the clay-pipe ceiling; book 2–4 weeks ahead). Budget version: Koreatown on 32nd, a 10-minute walk south, no reservation needed.

  • Late

    Rooftop drink at Bar SixtyFive, 65th floor of the same building (smart dress; no reservation for the bar est.).

Day 2 Downtown + Brooklyn — memorial, harbor, bridge, dusk on the Promenade

About 6 miles on foot · 2 boats · 1 bridge

  • 8:30 AM

    Subway to Fulton St 2345AC — coffee and a bagel at Leo's Bagels, 3 Hanover Sq, 6 min south, or grab-and-go inside the Oculus.

  • 9:00 AM

    9/11 Memorial pools (free, outdoors; names cut into bronze; the Survivor Tree). 9:30 AM museum slot ($36), 2 hours. If the visitor is a heavy feeler, do the pools only and give the museum's two hours to the harbor.

  • 11:30 AM

    The Oculus (free; the white ribs; 10 min), then One World Trade from the plaza. Skip its deck — you already have Top of the Rock.

  • 11:45 AM

    South on foot: Trinity Church → Wall Street (the NYSE façade, Fearless Girl) → the Federal Hall steps → Stone Street, cobbled, tables outside.

  • 12:30 PM

    Lunch on Stone Street: Adrienne's Pizzabar (the square pie) or Fraunces Tavern (Washington said goodbye to his officers upstairs, 1783). Skip the Charging Bull queue — a 30-second look from across Bowling Green is enough.

  • 1:30 PM

    Staten Island Ferry from Whitehall Terminal — free, every 30 min, 25 min each way. Right side going out for the statue; the open rear deck for the skyline coming back. You must get off and re-board at St. George (5 min). Back at Whitehall about 2:45.

    Swap: if a Liberty/Ellis slot is booked, make it the 9 AM boat from Battery Park and move the 9/11 block to 1:30 PM.

  • 3:00 PM

    Walk north 15 min up Broadway past City Hall to the Brooklyn Bridge pedestrian entrance at Centre & Chambers — or 456 one stop from Bowling Green to Brooklyn Bridge–City Hall.

  • 3:15 PMto 4:00 PM

    Walk the Brooklyn Bridge, Manhattan → Brooklyn. 1.1 miles on the wooden promenade; bikes were moved to the roadway in 2021, so no dodging. The light on the towers is best 3–5 PM in fall. Down the stairs at Washington St.

  • 4:00 PM

    DUMBO: the Manhattan Bridge framed on Washington St (the photo — 2 minutes, then move), Jane's Carousel, Brooklyn Bridge Park Pier 1 lawns and Pebble Beach; ice cream at OddFellows on Water St.

  • 5:15 PM

    Ten minutes uphill to the Brooklyn Heights Promenade — the skyline at dusk, the reason for the whole day. Bring a layer; it's windy.

  • 6:15 PM

    Dinner back down the hill: Juliana's, 19 Old Fulton St (Patsy Grimaldi's coal-oven pizza; walk-in only; arriving before 6:30 keeps the wait under 30 min) — or Time Out Market, 55 Water St, if the group wants different things.

  • 7:45 PM

    Home the scenic way: NYC Ferry from DUMBO (Fulton Landing) to Wall St/Pier 11 — $4.50, the cheapest skyline cruise in the city; buy in the NYC Ferry app. Raining or late: AC from High St, 2 stops to Fulton, 12 min.

  • Late

    Comedy Cellar, 117 MacDougal (booked) or a drink on Stone Street.

Day 3 Uptown to the West Side — the Met, the High Line, the Village, Broadway

About 7 miles on foot · 1 museum · 1 show

  • 8:30 AM

    Breakfast on the Upper West Side: Zabar's café, 2245 Broadway (80th) — lox on a bagel; then Levain Bakery, 351 Amsterdam (77th) for the 6-ounce cookie to carry into the park.

  • 9:30 AM

    Cross Central Park on foot from W 81st: Turtle Pond → Belvedere Castle (the little balcony over the pond) → the Obelisk → out at 82nd & Fifth. 40 min. The American Museum of Natural History is right there at the start — the kids swap.

  • 10:15 AMto 12:45 PM

    The Met, 1000 Fifth Ave — a route, not a wander: Temple of Dendur (Egyptian, gallery 131) → Arms & Armor → the American Wing courtyard (Tiffany windows, coffee) → European Paintings, 2nd floor (Vermeer, Rembrandt, Bruegel's Harvesters) → the Roof Garden if open (seasonal, usually Apr–Oct est.) for the skyline over the treeline. Hard stop at 2½ hours.

  • 12:45 PM

    Lunch: Lexington Candy Shop, 1226 Lexington (83rd) — a 1925 luncheonette; egg cream, burger, the real thing. Or E.A.T. on Madison.

  • 2:00 PM

    6 77th St → Grand Central, then 7 to 34 St–Hudson Yards, its last stop. About 30 min door to door. A cab is 20 min, about $25 est., if feet are done.

  • 2:45 PM

    Hudson Yards — glance at the Vessel (climbing is ticketed; not worth the time) → up onto the High Line at 30th St and walk it south: 1.45 miles, 60 min with stops — the 30th St spur, the 10th Ave amphitheater window over traffic, the sculptures. Down into Chelsea Market at 16th: Los Tacos No. 1 (adobada) as the 3:30 snack; Lobster Place if it's more.

  • 4:15 PM

    Out at Gansevoort → Little Island (Pier 55; free; the tulip pillars; 20 min) → optional Whitney Museum ($30 est.) if the Met didn't fill them up.

  • 5:00 PM

    West Village on foot: Gansevoort → Bleecker St (Murray's Cheese; skip the Magnolia line) → Grove & Bedford (the Friends building at 90 Bedford — one minute) → Washington Square Park: the arch, the chess tables, whoever is playing the piano under it.

  • 6:15 PM

    Dinner: Via Carota, 51 Grove (walk-in; at 5:30 or after 9:30 the wait is sane, before 7 it's an hour) · L'Artusi, 228 W 10th (book) · cheap and perfect: Mamoun's Falafel, 119 MacDougal ($5) or John's of Bleecker, 278 Bleecker (whole pies only; historically cash-only — carry $60).

  • 7:25 PM

    1 Christopher St → 50th St, 12 min → the 8 PM Broadway show you booked weeks ago. Doors 30 min before; most shows 7 or 8 PM; Wed/Sat matinees at 2.

  • Lateif no show

    Jazz at the Village Vanguard, 178 7th Ave S (sets 8 and 10 PM) or Smalls, 183 W 10th — you're already there.

A 4th day, or replace a block

Swap-in modules

  • Knicks at the Garden
    3 h · evening
    Oct 20 is banner night vs LeBron's 76ers — the 2026 champions get their rings — and it is priced like a final; any other home game is the better first-timer buy. Sit anywhere; the Chase Bridge seats hang over the court. Pregame: Koreatown is two blocks away.
  • MSG tour
    75 min · daytime
    $48. The locker-room level and the ice-to-court changeover story. Pair with Moynihan Train Hall across 8th Ave (free, 10 min).
  • Harlem Sunday
    half day
    Sunday service at a church that welcomes visitors (arrive 30 min early, dress properly, stay the full service — it isn't a show); lunch at Sylvia's or Red Rooster; the reopened Studio Museum in Harlem (late 2025 est.); the Apollo marquee on 125th.
  • Queens food crawl
    half day
    7 to Jackson Hts–Roosevelt Av: Colombian, Nepali momos, arepas along Roosevelt Ave — or stay on to Flushing–Main St: the New World Mall food court, Nan Xiang soup dumplings. The most real-New-York $30 of the trip.
  • Williamsburg
    half day
    L to Bedford Ave. Saturdays Apr–Nov: Smorgasburg in Marsha P. Johnson State Park (11 AM–6 PM est.). Domino Park's sugar-factory view, the Bedford Ave shops, Radegast beer hall; Lilia if booked a month out. Back by the East River NYC Ferry.
  • Roosevelt Island Tram
    45 min
    Same $3 tap. An aerial ride over the East River from 59th & 2nd; walk to Four Freedoms Park at the tip. The cheapest aerial view in the city.
  • The Frick · the Morgan · the Cloisters
    2 h each
    The Frick reopened in 2025 with its second floor open for the first time; the Morgan Library's 1906 room; the Cloisters (the Met's medieval branch, A to 190th) is the quietest place in Manhattan.
  • Tenement Museum
    90 min
    Lower East Side; guided only, book 1–2 weeks out; then Katz's (pastrami; keep the ticket) and Russ & Daughters (the lox counter, since 1914).
  • Coney Island
    half day
    Only while the rides run (they close after Halloween). Q to the end. Nathan's, the Cyclone, the boardwalk.
  • Baseball
    3 h
    The regular season ends in late September; October is playoff-only and pricey. Yankee Stadium (4D, 20 min from Midtown), Citi Field (7).

Adjust for

  • Parents 70+
    Taxis between blocks (a cab across Midtown is $12–18 est.); skip the bridge walk and take the ferry both ways (DUMBO ↔ Wall St); cut the Met to 90 min. Top of the Rock has elevators throughout; the Staten Island Ferry is fully step-free.
  • Kids
    American Museum of Natural History replaces the Met; Central Park Zoo and Wollman Rink; the Intrepid (a real carrier, a real Concorde); Jane's Carousel in DUMBO; FAO Schwarz at Rock Center; the Roosevelt Island tram counts as a ride.
  • One day only
    Day 1 as written, but replace MoMA with the Staten Island Ferry (1 from 50th to South Ferry, 25 min) and come back up for sunset at the Rock.
  • Five days
    Add the Queens crawl and Williamsburg; give the Liberty/Ellis ferry its own morning; leave one afternoon unscheduled in the Village.
  • Rain
    Grand Central → the Met (or MoMA) → Chelsea Market → the Oculus → Koreatown. All indoors, all connected by subway.

  • Fares
    $3 a ride; tap the same card or phone every time and after 12 taps in 7 rolling days rides are free ($35 cap). Free bus↔subway transfer within 2 hours. No MetroCard sales since Jan 1, 2026; cash on buses is ending this year.
  • Express vs local
    The map shows white dots (local only) and black dots (express stops). Wrong train and you sail past your stop; get off and cross the platform.
  • Entrances
    They say "Uptown" or "Downtown." Some entrances serve one direction only; read the sign before tapping in.
  • Distances
    20 north–south blocks ≈ 1 mile ≈ 20 min on foot. Avenue blocks are about 3× longer. Streets run east–west and count up going north; avenues run north–south and count up going west.
  • Sidewalk rules
    Walk on the right, stand right on escalators, never stop at the top of subway stairs, step out of the flow to look at your phone.
  • Taxis vs apps
    Yellow cabs are metered and take cards; JFK↔Manhattan is a flat ~$70 + tolls + tip est.. Ubers and Lyfts add a $1.50 congestion surcharge below 60th; cabs $0.75.
  • Driving in
    Don't. $9 to enter Manhattan below 60th St at peak, $2.25 overnight, plus parking at $40–70 a day est.. Park outside the zone or at the airport.
  • Citi Bike
    Day pass about $25 est.; the Hudson River Greenway and the Central Park loop are the safe rides.
  • Tipping
    18–20% at restaurants (20% is the default now), $1–2 a drink, 15–20% in cabs, $2–5 a bag for a bellhop. Counter service: the tip screen is optional; 10% is fine.
  • Cash
    Almost nowhere needs it. Carry $40 for carts, John's of Bleecker, and tips.

What a New Yorker knows and a visitor doesn't

First-timer rules

  1. One deck, one big museum, one show, one bridge. Doubling any of them is the most common regret.
  2. Eat standing up at least once a day: a slice folded in half, a bacon-egg-and-cheese from a bodega, a halal-cart plate, a bagel with schmear. That is the cuisine.
  3. Reservations drop 14–30 days out at 9–10 AM on Resy and OpenTable. Walk-ins work at 5:30 or after 9:30. Between 7 and 9 you will wait.
  4. Don't book the hop-on-hop-off bus, the Charging Bull line, the M&M store, or a "Statue of Liberty cruise" that doesn't stop at the island. The Staten Island Ferry does the cruise for free.
  5. Layers, not a coat. Subway platforms are 10°F warmer than the street; the Promenade and the ferry deck are 10°F colder.
  6. Wear the shoes you've already walked 8 miles in. Day 2 is about 6 miles; Day 3 about 7.
  7. Look up in Grand Central, the Chrysler lobby, the Oculus, the Woolworth lobby; look down on the High Line. Buildings here are meant to be seen from inside.
  8. Do the postcard things without apology — Times Square once, the bridge, the deck — then spend the equal time somewhere the visitor didn't know existed: the Whispering Gallery, Stone Street, the Promenade, Roosevelt Ave.
  9. Sundays: the Met opens 10 AM, the NYPL reading room 1 PM, TKTS 11 AM, Chinatown dim sum is packed by noon — make Sunday the Brooklyn or Harlem day.
  10. The subway runs all night. Late-night waits are 20 min; a cab home after midnight is money well spent.
