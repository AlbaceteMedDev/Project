# Sales Pitch Copy — "Go talk to them" (competitor concession)

**Situation:** Customer is considering a competitor (Miro3D). The move is the honest
broker: concede the case where they genuinely win, then reclaim the case where you do,
and close by making yourself the advisor rather than the vendor.

---

## Original

> Oh 100% and I would totally advise you to do so if that's the case. Even if you wanted
> to reach out to them now and see what they could do for you, I would encourage you to do
> so. At the end of the day, obviously I would prefer you to stick with me, but this is
> your business too and for a 2x2x2 wound even with Miro3Ds $65 per cc cost, your profit
> margin would be $446 per application compared to $303 even at the $20 lesser cost I
> charger per sqcm but you're limited to only billing LxW.

---

## Version A — Text / spoken (recommended)

Keeps your voice. Reads out loud the way you'd actually say it.

> Oh, 100% — and honestly, I'd encourage you to make that call. Reach out to them today if
> you want. I'd rather you have the full picture than sit there wondering about it.
>
> And I'll be straight with you: on a deep wound, they win. Take that 2x2x2. Miro3D bills
> by volume — length x width x depth — so even at $65 a cc, you'd clear about $446 an
> application. I bill by surface area, length x width only, so that same wound nets you
> around $303 — and that's with my cost running $20 less per sq cm. The depth just isn't
> billable on my side. No way around it.
>
> Where it flips is surface area. On a wide, shallow wound, all that depth math disappears
> and I'm the better number — and you're not paying $65 a cc to fill space.
>
> So my honest ask isn't "stick with me." It's: send me the dimensions before you order and
> I'll tell you which one pays you better, even when the answer is them. I'd rather be the
> guy you trust on that call than the guy who only ever has one answer.

## What changed and why

1. **Cut the triple concession.** The original says "go ahead" three times before saying
   anything. Concede once, cleanly, then move.
2. **Split the run-on close.** The last sentence carried four separate ideas. Each one now
   gets its own beat.
3. **Promoted the actual insight.** Volume billing vs. area billing is *the* reason for the
   $446/$303 gap — it was buried in a trailing subordinate clause. It's now the headline of
   the argument.
4. **Made the math legible.** "$446 vs $303" lands only when the reader can see where the
   numbers come from.
5. **Added the reclaim.** The original concedes and stops, which hands over the frame. Naming
   the wide/shallow case where you win costs nothing — it's true — and makes the concession
   read as expertise rather than surrender.
6. **Added a close.** "Send me the dimensions and I'll tell you which one to use" converts a
   lost order into a standing reason to call you first. That's the durable position.
7. **Fixes:** "charger" → "charge"; "Miro3Ds" → "Miro3D's"; "sqcm" → "sq cm".

## Two flags before you send

- **Verify the figures.** $446, $303, $65/cc, and the $20 delta are yours — I preserved them
  exactly and didn't recompute anything. Since the whole message is built on that comparison,
  it's worth confirming against current reimbursement before it goes out in writing.
- **Consider a clinical line.** The message is entirely economics. One sentence on which
  product you'd actually pick for a deep wound clinically would strengthen it — and a
  written message that discusses only provider margin in product selection is a worse
  document to have on file than one that leads with fit.
