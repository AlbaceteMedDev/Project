# Clinical Reference

Measurement math, healing benchmarks, nutrition targets, the impediment matrix,
the infection framework, and the pre-signature audit checklist.

---

## 1. Measurement — do it the same way every week

Inconsistent technique produces fake trends. Fix the method and the numbers mean
something.

**Length** — longest dimension head-to-toe (12 o'clock to 6 o'clock).
**Width** — greatest dimension perpendicular to length (9 to 3 o'clock).
**Depth** — deepest point, moistened sterile swab, mark with gloved finger at
skin level, measure against the ruler. Note *where* the deepest point is.
**Undermining / tunneling** — clock positions with 12 o'clock toward the patient's
head. Record as "undermining 1.8 cm from 2 to 5 o'clock," "tunneling 3.2 cm at
7 o'clock."

Measure **before** debridement and again **after**. Debridement commonly makes a
wound bigger by removing non-viable tissue that was masking the true margin —
that is an expected finding, not a decline, and the note must say so explicitly
or the next reviewer will read it as deterioration.

### Area and volume

| Quantity | Formula | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Area (standard) | `L × W` | Rectangular convention. This is what most EMRs and payer policies expect. Use it as your reported number. |
| Area (elliptical) | `(π ÷ 4) × L × W` = `0.785 × L × W` | Closer to true area for oval wounds; ~21% smaller than L×W. Report it only if you report it every week. |
| Volume (estimate) | `L × W × D` | Crude but useful for deep wounds where area alone hides progress. |

### Percent change

```
Week-over-week  %  =  (previous area − current area) ÷ previous area × 100
Cumulative      %  =  (baseline area − current area) ÷ baseline area × 100
```

A positive number is reduction. Report **both** — a wound can have a flat week
inside an excellent overall trajectory, and the cumulative figure is what
supports continued treatment.

**Worked example.** Baseline 6.0 × 4.5 cm = 27.00 sq cm. Last week 4.8 × 3.6 =
17.28 sq cm. Today 4.2 × 3.1 = 13.02 sq cm.
Week-over-week: (17.28 − 13.02) ÷ 17.28 × 100 = **24.7% reduction**.
Cumulative: (27.00 − 13.02) ÷ 27.00 × 100 = **51.8% reduction from baseline**.

## 2. Healing trajectory benchmarks

These are the thresholds that separate "continue current care" from "this is
stalled, change something" — and they are also the thresholds payer policies are
built around.

| Benchmark | Threshold | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4-week area reduction, diabetic foot ulcer | ≥ 50% (Sheehan: ≥ 53%) | Strong predictor of healing by 12 weeks. Below it, healing is unlikely on the current plan. |
| 4-week area reduction, venous leg ulcer | ≥ 40% | Predicts healing by 24 weeks. Below it, reassess compression adequacy and perfusion first. |
| 4-week area reduction, pressure injury | ≥ 30–40% | Below it, the usual culprit is unrelieved pressure — recheck the offloading before changing the dressing. |
| Standard-care trial before advanced therapy | ≥ 30 days documented | Most CTP/skin-substitute policies require documented failure of standard care over ~30 days. Your weekly measurements *are* that documentation. |

**The stalled-wound statement.** When a wound misses its benchmark, the note must
say so and say what changed. A note that records four weeks of flat measurements
without a plan change is the single most common reason advanced therapy gets
denied — and clinically it means four weeks were lost.

> Wound has achieved only [X]% area reduction over [N] weeks of documented
> standard care ([describe: offloading, moist wound healing, serial selective
> debridement, compression, glycemic and nutritional optimization]), falling short
> of the ≥[40/50]% four-week benchmark predictive of healing. Wound is
> characterized as **stalled**. Reassessment of impediments completed this visit
> (below); plan escalated to [intervention] this date.

## 3. Wound characteristics — the vocabulary to chart in

**Tissue composition** — must total 100%.

- **Granulation** — beefy red, pale pink (poor perfusion or over-moist),
  dusky/dark red (poor perfusion or infection), friable/bleeds easily (bacterial
  burden), hypergranulation (above the level of surrounding skin; consider
  pressure, moisture imbalance, or foreign body).
- **Slough** — yellow/tan, adherent vs loosely adherent, stringy vs thick.
- **Eschar** — black/brown, dry and hard vs boggy/soft. *Stable dry eschar on an
  ischemic heel is left intact* — document that decision and why.
- **Epithelial** — new pink/pearly tissue advancing from the edges.

**Edges**

- **Attached and advancing** — the goal. Epithelium migrating over the bed.
- **Non-advancing / cliffed** — stalled at the same margin week over week.
- **Epibole (rolled edges)** — epithelium curls under and seals the edge to
  itself, halting migration. Requires edge debridement or chemical cautery to
  reopen. Chart it explicitly; it is a specific, actionable finding, not a
  descriptor.
- **Fibrotic** — firm, white, indurated, poorly vascularized rim.
- **Callused / hyperkeratotic** — typical of plantar wounds under pressure.
- **Undermined** — edge separated from the base.

**Periwound (chart within 4 cm of the edge)**

- **Maceration** — white, soft, waterlogged. Means the dressing is not managing
  exudate — change absorption, not antibiotics.
- **Excoriation** — denuded from moisture or adhesive stripping.
- **Erythema** — state whether blanchable or non-blanchable, and the distance
  from the edge in cm. Non-blanchable erythema extending > 2 cm is an infection
  finding.
- **Induration** — firmness on palpation, in cm from the edge.
- **Warmth / temperature difference** vs the contralateral or adjacent area.
- **Dry, scaly, hyperkeratotic** or **fluctuance**.

**Exudate** — amount (none / scant / small / moderate / large / copious), type
(serous / serosanguineous / sanguineous / purulent), and odor **after** cleansing
(odor before cleansing usually reflects the old dressing, not the wound).

**Pain** — 0–10 at rest, with dressing change, and during debridement; note
premedication given, agent, dose, and time.

## 4. Wound healing impediments — identify, intervene, educate, document

Every impediment gets three lines in the note: **what you found**, **what you did
about it**, and **who you taught**. The middle line is what makes it care rather
than observation.

| Impediment | How you establish it | Intervention documented | Education documented |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Unrelieved pressure** (hip/trochanter, sacrum, heel) | Ulcer over bony prominence; turn log review; erythema pattern matching support surface | Q2H repositioning in bed / Q1H in chair; 30° lateral tilt, **never 90° directly on the trochanter**; heels floated off the surface entirely; HOB ≤ 30°; pressure-redistribution mattress and wheelchair cushion; never position on the ulcer | Turn schedule posted at bedside; caregiver and facility nurse taught the 30° tilt with pillow bridging; teach-back obtained; turn log to be initialed each turn and reviewed at next visit |
| **Immobility / contracture** | Unable to self-reposition; Braden mobility subscore; observed ROM | Caregiver-assisted passive/active-assisted ROM to bilateral lower extremities, [N] reps × [N] sessions/day; PT/OT referral; chair-based mobility program | Caregiver taught heel slides, ankle pumps, hip/knee flexion-extension; return demonstration performed |
| **Poor perfusion / venous congestion** | Pulses, capillary refill, ABI, dependent rubor, edema, pooling | Compression [type, mmHg] applied — **verify ABI before compressing**; leg elevation above heart [N]× daily for [N] min; ankle pumps and leg raises to drive the calf pump; vascular referral if ABI < 0.9 or non-healing | Patient instructed **not to remove or loosen compression** between visits and told exactly whom to call if it becomes painful, numb, or discolored; taught leg raises and ankle pumps to encourage venous return and reduce pooling; teach-back obtained |
| **Malnutrition / low albumin** | Albumin, prealbumin, weight trend, intake %, MST/MNA screen | Protein target [g/day] with named food and supplement options; calorie target; vitamin C and zinc course; dietitian referral; weekly weights | Patient and caregiver given the specific protein plan in writing; caregiver taught to record intake |
| **Infection / biofilm** | NERDS/STONEES (§5); tissue culture; probe-to-bone | Serial debridement to disrupt biofilm; antimicrobial dressing; systemic antibiotics **only when clinical criteria are met**; imaging if osteomyelitis suspected | Signs to report between visits, with a named contact and threshold ("call if…") |
| **Moisture / incontinence** | Maceration, excoriation, IAD pattern | Barrier film or paste; toileting schedule; absorbent product change frequency; check-and-change protocol | Caregiver taught barrier application and the check-and-change interval |
| **Uncontrolled hyperglycemia** | A1c, CBG log | Realistic individualized target; med adjustment communicated to the managing clinician | Patient/caregiver taught the CBG log and when to call |
| **Medications impairing healing** | Chart review: systemic steroids, chemo/immunosuppressants, anticoagulants, NSAIDs | Discussed taper or alternative with the prescriber; documented the conversation and the decision | Patient told why the medication matters to the wound |
| **Smoking / nicotine** | History | Cessation counseling, duration, pharmacotherapy offered | Documented counseling time and patient response |
| **Non-adherence / caregiver burden** | Dressing found off, compression removed, turn log blank | Simplify the regimen to what is actually achievable; add home health visit frequency; address caregiver capacity directly | Barrier identified and named in the note, plus the concrete accommodation made |

## 5. Infection assessment and the platelet question

### The framework to chart in

**NERDS** — three or more suggests **superficial critical colonization**; treat
topically.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **N** | **N**on-healing wound |
| **E** | **E**xudate increased |
| **R** | **R**ed, friable granulation tissue |
| **D** | **D**ebris / slough in the wound bed |
| **S** | **S**mell after cleansing |

**STONEES** — three or more suggests **deep and surrounding tissue infection**;
systemic antibiotics indicated.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | **S**ize increasing |
| **T** | **T**emperature elevated (local warmth or systemic fever) |
| **O** | **O**s — probes to or exposed bone |
| **N** | **N**ew areas of breakdown |
| **E** | **E**xudate |
| **E** | **E**rythema / **E**dema (cellulitis) |
| **S** | **S**mell |

### Culture technique

Swab cultures of an uncleansed wound grow surface colonizers and mislead. Cleanse
with saline first, debride, then either take **tissue** or use the **Levine
technique** (rotate the swab over 1 sq cm of clean granulating tissue with enough
pressure to express fluid). Chart which technique you used — it changes how the
result should be read.

### Thrombocytopenia — how to document it defensibly

A low platelet count is a real finding that deserves workup. It is **not**, on its
own, evidence of wound infection, and a note that starts antibiotics on that basis
is difficult to defend clinically or on review.

- The platelet response to acute infection and inflammation is usually a *rise*
  (thrombocytosis as an acute-phase reactant), not a fall.
- Thrombocytopenia **does** accompany severe systemic infection and sepsis, where
  it reflects consumption or DIC — a marker of severity in a patient who is
  clinically septic, not a screening test for a colonized wound.
- In a chronic-wound patient, the more common causes are medication effect
  (heparin/HIT, linezolid, valproate, sulfonamides, PPIs), liver disease with
  splenic sequestration, chronic alcohol use, B12 or folate deficiency, marrow
  suppression, myelodysplasia, or ITP.

**So document it this way:** report the value and the trend, state the
differential you considered, order the workup, and — separately — make the
antibiotic decision on clinical infection findings (NERDS/STONEES), the culture,
and inflammatory markers. Then the platelet count *supports* the picture instead
of carrying it.

> Platelet count [X] K/µL, down from [Y] K/µL on [date]. Reviewed in the context
> of the wound assessment above, which meets [N] of 7 STONEES criteria
> ([list]) — findings consistent with deep and surrounding tissue infection.
> Thrombocytopenia in this setting raises concern for a systemic inflammatory
> response with platelet consumption; differential also includes medication
> effect ([review: heparin, linezolid, valproate, sulfonamides, PPI]), hepatic
> disease with sequestration, and nutritional deficiency (B12/folate), given the
> concurrent hypoalbuminemia. Ordered: repeat CBC with differential and
> peripheral smear, CRP and ESR, CMP, B12/folate, and medication reconciliation.
> [Tissue culture / Levine-technique culture] obtained today. Given the clinical
> infection findings, empiric [antibiotic, dose, route, duration] initiated with
> coverage for [organisms], to be narrowed on culture results. Reassessment of
> both clinical response and platelet count on [date]. If platelets continue to
> fall or the patient develops systemic signs, will [escalate: hematology
> referral / transfer for evaluation].

## 6. Nutrition — turning "low albumin" into a prescription

Documenting a low albumin without a plan is an observation. The plan is what
counts.

**Read the albumin correctly.** Albumin and prealbumin are negative acute-phase
reactants — they fall in inflammation regardless of intake, and a chronic wound is
an inflammatory state. Chart albumin alongside **weight trend, intake percentage,
and a screening tool** so the malnutrition finding stands on its own.

### Targets

| Target | Amount | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protein | **1.25–1.5 g/kg/day** (up to 2.0 g/kg/day for large or multiple wounds, if renal function allows) | NPIAP/EPUAP/PPPIA guidance for adults with a pressure injury at risk of malnutrition |
| Calories | **30–35 kcal/kg/day** | Same |
| Fluid | **1 mL per kcal**, adjusted for cardiac and renal status | Standard |
| Vitamin C | 500 mg twice daily (if deficiency suspected or intake poor) | Collagen synthesis cofactor |
| Zinc | Elemental 50 mg daily, **14 days maximum** | Prolonged zinc induces copper deficiency — always chart a stop date |
| Arginine/glutamine/HMB supplement | 1–2 servings daily | Evidence supports use in pressure injury; specify the product |

**Do the arithmetic in the note.** For a 70 kg patient: 70 × 1.5 = **105 g protein
per day**. Then show how the patient gets there — that is the part a dietitian,
a nurse, and a family member can actually execute.

### Named food options (grams of protein per serving)

| Food | Protein | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whey or soy protein shake | 25–30 g | Highest yield per volume; best for poor appetite |
| Oral nutrition supplement, high-protein (e.g., 8 oz) | 20 g | Between meals, not with meals — it displaces intake |
| Greek yogurt, 1 cup | 17–20 g | |
| Cottage cheese, 1 cup | 24 g | |
| Canned tuna or chicken, 3 oz | 22–25 g | |
| Chicken breast, 3 oz | 26 g | |
| Eggs, 2 large | 12 g | Easy to chew, cheap, well tolerated |
| Milk fortified with 2 Tbsp dry milk powder, 8 oz | 14 g | Adds protein without adding volume — good for early satiety |
| Peanut butter, 2 Tbsp | 7 g | |
| Cheese, 1 oz | 7 g | |

**Volume is usually the constraint, not willingness.** For patients with early
satiety or poor appetite, fortify what they already eat (milk powder into soups,
mashed potatoes, oatmeal; protein powder into shakes) rather than adding meals.
Chart the strategy, not just the number.

## 7. Dressing selection logic — the "why this dressing" sentence

Payers and colleagues both want the reasoning, and it is one sentence.

| Wound state | Choose | Because |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy exudate | Alginate, hydrofiber, foam, ABD pad | Absorb and protect the periwound from maceration |
| Moderate exudate | Foam, hydrofiber | Balance moisture |
| Minimal / dry | Hydrogel, amorphous gel, moisture-donating | Autolytic debridement needs moisture |
| Slough / necrosis | Enzymatic, hydrogel, or sharp debridement | Remove the barrier to granulation |
| Critical colonization | Silver, PHMB, iodine, methylene blue | Reduce bioburden without systemic exposure |
| Clean granulating bed | Non-adherent contact layer (e.g., Adaptic) | Protect new tissue; avoid disrupting granulation at change |
| Fragile / painful | Silicone contact layer | Atraumatic removal |
| Undermining / tunneling | Loosely packed rope or ribbon | Fill dead space without over-packing |
| Periwound maceration | Barrier film or paste + more absorptive primary | Fix the cause, not the symptom |

## 8. Pre-signature audit checklist

Run this before you sign. Anything unchecked is either missing care or missing
documentation.

**Every visit**
- [ ] Measurements recorded (L × W × D, undermining/tunneling by clock position)
- [ ] Area calculated, week-over-week **and** cumulative % change stated
- [ ] Tissue percentages total 100%
- [ ] Edges, periwound, exudate, and odor characterized
- [ ] Week-over-week comparison narrative present (not just numbers)
- [ ] Trajectory stated: on track / stalled — with the benchmark named
- [ ] Impediments listed, each with an intervention **and** the education given
- [ ] Turning/offloading schedule specified and adherence reviewed
- [ ] Comorbidity control addressed with a realistic target and who manages it
- [ ] Labs reviewed with an action attached to each abnormal value
- [ ] Education documented by name, content, method, and **teach-back**
- [ ] Communication with facility nursing / caregiver / PCP documented
- [ ] Plan with a specific next-visit date and what would change the plan

**When debridement is performed**
- [ ] Informed consent obtained and documented **before** the procedure
- [ ] Indication stated (what non-viable tissue, why it must come off)
- [ ] Anesthesia: agent, concentration, amount — or explicitly "none required"
- [ ] Instrument named **with size** (e.g., 4 mm curette, #15 blade)
- [ ] Tissue type removed named (slough, eschar, non-viable subcutaneous, fibrotic edge)
- [ ] **Deepest tissue level removed** stated — this drives code selection
- [ ] Area debrided in **sq cm** and as a **% of the wound**
- [ ] Bleeding described and hemostasis method documented with duration
- [ ] Patient tolerance and pain score during the procedure
- [ ] Post-debridement wound bed described (% granulation after)
- [ ] Post-debridement measurements recorded
- [ ] Any increase in size explained as expected unmasking, if applicable
- [ ] Complications and estimated blood loss stated
- [ ] Selective (97597/97598) vs excisional (11042–11047) supported by the words used

**When a wound matrix / CTP is applied**
- [ ] Failure of ≥ 30 days of documented conservative care summarized
- [ ] Wound bed documented as adequately prepared (granulating, no active infection, no necrotic tissue)
- [ ] Informed consent: product named, purpose, **alternatives**, risks, benefits, questions answered, who consented and their capacity or authority
- [ ] Product name, manufacturer, **lot number**, size applied, expiration
- [ ] Amount used **and amount discarded/wasted**, in sq cm
- [ ] Application technique described per IFU
- [ ] Secondary dressing stack documented in order
- [ ] Application number in the series (e.g., "application 2 of a planned course")
- [ ] Post-application instructions and the reassessment date

## 9. Terminology precision that changes the record

| Say this | Not this | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Non-viable tissue removed to the level of subcutaneous tissue" | "Cleaned up the wound" | Depth of tissue removed drives code selection |
| "Punctate capillary bleeding, hemostasis with direct pressure × 3 minutes" | "Bled a little" | Bleeding at a viable base is the endpoint of debridement, not a complication |
| "Undermining 1.8 cm from 2 to 5 o'clock" | "Some undermining" | Reproducible next week |
| "Epibole present at the 3 to 6 o'clock margin" | "Edges look funny" | Names a finding with a specific intervention |
| "Non-blanchable erythema extending 3 cm from the wound edge" | "Redness around it" | Blanchable vs not, and the distance, are the clinical decision |
| "Wound stalled: 12% reduction over 4 weeks against a ≥ 40% benchmark" | "Slow to heal" | This is the sentence advanced therapy coverage rests on |
| "Patient verbalized understanding and demonstrated 30° lateral tilt with pillow bridging" | "Educated patient" | Teach-back is the documentation standard |
| "Wound enlarged 1.2 sq cm following debridement, consistent with expected unmasking of the true margin after removal of adherent slough" | (silence) | Prevents the next reader from charting deterioration |
