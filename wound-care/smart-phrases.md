# Smart Phrases — Copy / Paste Library

Drop these into your EMR as dot-phrases. Bracketed `[text]` is the only thing you
change. Each block is written to stand on its own in a chart.

Suggested trigger names are given as `.wcXXX` — rename to your own convention.

---

## Turning and offloading

**`.wcturn`** — the repositioning order

> Repositioning order: **every 2 hours in bed around the clock, and every 1 hour
> when in the chair with a small assisted weight shift every 15 minutes.**
> Positioning is to be at a **30° lateral tilt with pillow bridging behind the
> back — 90° side-lying is specifically prohibited**, as it places full body
> weight directly onto the [greater trochanter / sacrum] where the ulcer is
> located. **No positioning on the [right/left] side** until the ulcer has
> healed. **Heels are to be floated completely free of the mattress** with a
> pillow supporting the length of the calves and the knees slightly flexed. Head
> of bed at or below 30° except during meals, to limit shear. A **turn log is
> posted at the bedside to be initialed at each turn** and will be reviewed by
> this provider at every visit.

**`.wcturnadh`** — adherence review, gaps found

> Turn log reviewed at the bedside. Documented repositioning is occurring at
> [actual] intervals rather than the ordered [ordered] interval, with **[N]
> documented gaps of ≥ [N] hours** between [dates], predominantly on [shift].
> [The patient was found positioned directly on the ulcer at the time of this
> visit and was repositioned by this provider before assessment.] The barrier
> identified by [name, role] is [barrier]. I discussed this directly with [name,
> role] and **escalated to [name, DON/administrator] by [method] at [time]**,
> requesting [specific action]. Unrelieved pressure is the determining factor for
> this wound, and no dressing change will compensate for it.

**`.wcturnadhgood`** — adherence review, compliant

> Turn log reviewed at the bedside and is complete, with repositioning documented
> at the ordered q2h interval and no gaps exceeding [N] hours since the last
> visit. Positioning technique observed and correct. [Name, role] and the
> caregiver were acknowledged for consistent adherence, which is reflected in the
> wound's trajectory.

## Comorbidity control — realistic targets

**`.wccomorb`**

> Comorbidity control was reviewed with attention to what is **realistically
> achievable for this patient** rather than to standard guideline targets.
> [Condition] is currently [status]; the individualized target is **[target]**,
> which is [less stringent than / consistent with] the general guideline because
> [age, frailty, life expectancy, hypoglycemia or fall risk, functional
> limitation]. This is managed by [clinician], and I communicated [content] by
> [method] on [date]. Where full control is not attainable, the achievable target
> and the reason are stated so that the plan reflects this patient rather than a
> generic goal.

**`.wcdm`** — diabetes, older adult

> Type 2 diabetes with A1c [X]% ([trend from prior]). **Individualized glycemic
> target of < [8.0]%** is appropriate for this [age]-year-old patient with
> [comorbidities, functional limitation, hypoglycemia risk]; tighter control to
> 6.5–7.0% is **not** an appropriate goal here and would increase hypoglycemia
> and fall risk without wound benefit. Communicated to [PCP] on [date]; A1c to be
> reassessed [interval].

## Perfusion, compression, and venous return

**`.wccompress`**

> Perfusion assessed prior to compression: DP [__], PT [__], capillary refill
> [N] seconds, **ABI [X] on [date]** — perfusion is adequate and [full-strength
> 30–40 mmHg / modified 23–30 mmHg] compression is safe. [Type] compression
> applied at [mmHg] with graduated reduction proximally.
>
> **The patient was instructed not to remove or loosen the compression wrap
> between visits.** I explained the reason in plain terms: when the wrap comes
> off, blood pools in the leg, the tissue swells, oxygen and nutrients cannot
> reach the wound, and the progress made during the week is lost. She was told
> that a snug sensation is expected, and was given the specific findings that
> mean she should **call rather than remove it** — numbness, toes changing color,
> new or increasing pain, or inability to move the toes — along with the office
> number and the after-hours number.
>
> **Leg raises prescribed, [10] repetitions [4] times daily**, to activate the
> calf muscle pump and drive venous return, decreasing blood pooling in the lower
> leg. **Ankle pumps, [20] repetitions each hour while awake.** **Elevation above
> heart level for [30] minutes, [3] times daily**, with the heel supported and
> free of pressure. [Caregiver] was taught to assist with and prompt all three,
> as the patient will not initiate them independently.

## Nutrition and the low albumin

**`.wcalb`**

> **Albumin [X] g/dL** (prior [Y] on [date]). Low and [falling/stable],
> consistent with protein-calorie malnutrition. **Interpretation documented:**
> albumin is a negative acute-phase reactant and falls with inflammation
> independent of intake — with a CRP of [X] and an [inflamed/infected] wound,
> some of this decline is inflammatory rather than nutritional. The malnutrition
> diagnosis is therefore supported independently by **[X]% weight loss over [N]
> weeks ([prior] → [current] kg), approximately [X]% meal completion by report,
> and a prealbumin of [X]**. Hypoalbuminemia also contributes to third-spacing
> and peripheral edema, further impairing perfusion at the wound site.
>
> **Protein prescription: [weight] kg × 1.5 g/kg/day = [N] g protein per day.**
> Calories [weight] × [32] = **[N] kcal/day.** Renal function [permits this
> loading (Cr [X]) / requires modification]. Current estimated intake [N] g/day —
> **gap of approximately [N] g/day.**
>
> **How the gap will be closed** (specific options given in writing, since "eat
> more protein" has already been tried):
> — **Two high-protein oral supplements daily ([20] g each = [40] g), given
> between meals rather than with them**, so they add to intake instead of
> displacing it. [Patient has refused the current product; [N] alternative
> flavors and a clear-liquid option were offered and [N] selected for trial.]
> — **Fortify foods already accepted** rather than adding volume, given early
> satiety: 2 Tbsp dry milk powder into oatmeal, soup, mashed potatoes, and milk
> (+14 g per fortified 8 oz serving).
> — **High-yield, low-volume foods the patient reports liking:** Greek yogurt
> 1 cup (17 g), cottage cheese 1 cup (24 g), two eggs (12 g), 3 oz canned tuna
> (25 g), 3 oz chicken (26 g), 1 oz cheese (7 g), 2 Tbsp peanut butter (7 g).
> — **Arginine/glutamine/HMB supplement, [2] servings daily.**
> — **Vitamin C 500 mg BID. Zinc sulfate 220 mg daily (50 mg elemental) — STOP
> DATE [date]**, limited to 14 days, as prolonged zinc induces copper deficiency.
> — **Registered dietitian consult ordered. Weekly weights**, same scale, same
> time of day.
> — Repeat albumin, prealbumin, and CRP on [date], to be **interpreted together**:
> a falling CRP with a rising prealbumin is the pattern that confirms nutritional
> response rather than resolving inflammation alone.

## Thrombocytopenia

**`.wcplt`** — full version with differential

> **Platelets [X] K/µL, down from [Y] K/µL on [date] ([Z]% decline).** New
> thrombocytopenia, [mild/moderate/severe].
>
> **Differential considered:**
> 1. **Consumption in the setting of systemic infection.** This patient has
>    clinical deep tissue infection ([N]/7 STONEES: [list]), [fever], leukocytosis
>    [X] with [Y]% neutrophils, and a CRP that has [risen from A to B]. In severe
>    infection platelets fall through consumption. **The platelet count is being
>    read as a marker of systemic inflammatory burden and severity — not as the
>    evidence of infection**, which is established by the wound findings, the
>    fever, the leukocytosis, and the CRP/ESR.
> 2. **Medication effect** — patient is on [enoxaparin, pantoprazole, linezolid,
>    valproate, sulfonamide], each associated with thrombocytopenia. **4T score
>    for HIT: [N] points ([low/intermediate/high] probability)** — [fall is [X]%,
>    timing, absence of thrombosis, alternative cause present]. [Agent] [continued
>    with daily platelet monitoring / discontinued].
> 3. **Nutritional deficiency** — B12 and folate ordered given documented
>    malnutrition and hypoalbuminemia.
> 4. **Hepatic disease with splenic sequestration** — [history], LFTs ordered.
>
> **Ordered today:** repeat CBC with differential **and peripheral smear** (to
> assess for schistocytes and to exclude pseudothrombocytopenia), CMP with LFTs,
> B12, folate, PT/INR and fibrinogen [to evaluate for DIC], and medication
> reconciliation with the facility pharmacist.
> **Reassessment:** CBC [frequency] then [date]. **Escalation:** if platelets fall
> below [100] K/µL, if the smear shows schistocytes, or if bleeding develops →
> hematology consultation and reconsideration of [anticoagulant].

**`.wcpltabx`** — linking to the antibiotic decision, correctly

> The decision to initiate systemic antibiotics rests on the clinical infection
> findings — **[N]/7 STONEES criteria ([list])**, [fever of X], leukocytosis of
> [X] with [Y]% neutrophils, CRP [X], ESR [X], and [positive probe-to-bone /
> culture]. The concurrent thrombocytopenia is **consistent with** this picture
> as a marker of systemic inflammatory burden and is being followed as a severity
> signal with its own workup, **but it is not the basis for the antibiotic
> decision.** [Tissue / Levine-technique] culture obtained today. Empiric
> **[antibiotic, dose, route, frequency]** started, covering [organisms], **to be
> narrowed on sensitivities** — culture follow-up [date]. Reassessment of clinical
> response [date].

## Infection assessment

**`.wcnerds`**

> **NERDS** (superficial — ≥3 suggests critical colonization): Non-healing
> [Y/N] · Exudate increased [Y/N] · Red friable granulation [Y/N] · Debris [Y/N]
> · Smell [Y/N] = **[N]/5.**
> **STONEES** (deep — ≥3 suggests deep and surrounding infection): Size
> increasing [Y/N] · Temperature elevated [Y/N] · Os, probes to bone [Y/N] · New
> breakdown [Y/N] · Exudate [Y/N] · Erythema/Edema [Y/N] · Smell [Y/N] =
> **[N]/7.**
> **Impression:** [no clinical infection / critical colonization — topical
> therapy indicated / **deep and surrounding tissue infection — systemic therapy
> indicated**].

**`.wcculture`**

> Culture obtained by **[tissue biopsy from the wound base / Levine technique —
> swab rotated over 1 sq cm of clean granulating tissue with sufficient pressure
> to express fluid]**, after cleansing with normal saline and after debridement,
> to sample viable tissue rather than surface colonizers. Sent for aerobic,
> anaerobic, and fungal culture with sensitivities. Follow-up [date].

## Debridement

**`.wcdebconsent`**

> **Informed consent for debridement.** The procedure, its purpose, alternatives
> (enzymatic debridement, continued dressing therapy alone, and no treatment),
> and its risks — pain, bleeding, infection, the need for repeat procedures, and
> **the likelihood that the measured wound would appear larger afterward once
> non-viable tissue is removed** — were discussed with [patient / name,
> relationship, authorized representative], who [demonstrates capacity to
> participate in this decision]. Questions were invited and answered.
> **[Verbal/written] consent was obtained prior to the procedure.** Site verified;
> timeout performed.

**`.wcdeb`** — the procedure

> **Indication:** [X]% [adherent slough / eschar / fibrotic edge / callus]
> obstructing the wound bed [in the setting of clinical infection]. Debridement
> is medically necessary to remove non-viable tissue, disrupt biofilm, [obtain a
> valid tissue culture,] and permit accurate assessment of the wound base.
>
> **Anesthesia:** [Topical lidocaine 4%, approximately [N] g, [N]-minute dwell /
> none required — wound bed insensate / patient declined].
> **Technique:** **[Selective, non-excisional — removal of devitalized tissue
> only, without excision into viable tissue / Sharp excisional — removal
> extending into viable tissue]**.
> **Instruments:** **[4] mm sterile ring curette** [; **#15 scalpel blade** to
> the fibrotic margin at [__] o'clock; Adson forceps with iris scissors].
> **Tissue removed:** [adherent slough, soft eschar, fibrotic non-viable tissue
> at the margin, callus].
> **Deepest tissue level removed:** [devitalized tissue only, no viable tissue
> excised / subcutaneous tissue / fascia / muscle / bone].
> **Area debrided: [N] sq cm = [X]% of the [A] sq cm wound surface.**
> **Endpoint:** carried to a viable base as evidenced by punctate capillary
> bleeding across the debrided surface.
>
> **Bleeding and hemostasis: the wound bled during debridement** — [punctate
> capillary / moderate] bleeding, **expected and desirable as it confirms a
> viable base**, most notable at [location] where [instrument] was used.
> **Hemostasis was achieved with direct pressure using saline-moistened gauze for
> [N] minutes**, followed by [calcium alginate applied for its hemostatic
> property / silver nitrate applied to [site] / epinephrine-soaked gauze].
> **Hemostasis was confirmed complete before any dressing was applied.**
> **Estimated blood loss: [minimal, less than [N] mL].**
>
> **Tolerance:** Patient tolerated the procedure [well / fairly]. **Pain [N]/10
> during the procedure** [, down from [N]/10 at dressing removal following topical
> anesthetic]. [The procedure was paused [N] times at the patient's request and
> resumed after brief rest.] No distress. Vital signs stable throughout.
> **Specimen:** [None / tissue from the wound base sent for aerobic, anaerobic,
> and fungal culture with sensitivities].
> **Complications:** [None].

**`.wcdebpost`** — post-debridement reassessment

> **Post-debridement reassessment.** Wound bed now **[X]% granulation [— beefy
> red, uniform, no slough, no eschar, no fibrinous coating]**, [X]% slough
> remaining [at [location], deliberately left in place pending [reason]], [X]%
> eschar.
> **Post-debridement measurements: [L] × [W] × [D] cm = [A] sq cm.**
> **Change from pre-debridement: [increased/decreased] [N] sq cm.**

**`.wcunmask`** — the sentence that prevents a misread next week

> The measured wound enlarged by [N] sq cm following debridement. **This is
> expected unmasking of the true wound margin after removal of adherent
> non-viable tissue [and release of the rolled edge] and does NOT represent
> clinical deterioration.** Next week's comparison will be made against the
> post-debridement measurement of [A] sq cm.

## Advanced therapy — consent and application

**`.wcmlconsent`** — informed consent

> **Informed consent for [Microlyte Ag / tri-membrane wrap].** Discussed with
> [patient], who is alert, oriented, and demonstrates capacity to participate in
> this decision, [with [name], [relationship], present at the patient's request].
>
> **What it is and why:** I explained that [Microlyte Ag] is a very thin,
> flexible antimicrobial wound matrix [containing silver] that is laid directly
> onto a clean, granulating wound bed to support new tissue growth and help
> control bacteria at the surface. It is recommended because this wound has
> achieved only **[X]% area reduction over [N] weeks** against a ≥ [40/50]%
> benchmark despite [N] weeks of documented [compression, offloading, debridement,
> and dressing care] — the wound is clean, well-perfused, and uninfected, but
> stalled.
>
> **Alternatives discussed:** continuing the current dressing regimen alone,
> trying a different advanced dressing or cellular/tissue product, and no further
> treatment. The likely course of each was described, including that continuing
> unchanged would most likely mean continued slow or absent progress based on the
> last [N] weeks.
>
> **Risks discussed:** I told the patient the product is low-risk and
> non-invasive, and that **no systemic risks are expected with topical
> application**. The specific risks reviewed were: local irritation or discomfort
> at the site; sensitivity or allergic reaction to the product or its silver
> component; **infection if the dressing becomes contaminated, gets wet, or if
> sterile technique is broken during a change**; the product failing to adhere or
> migrating, requiring reapplication; and **the possibility that the wound does
> not improve, in which case we would stop and change approach.** The patient was
> told the product does not guarantee healing.
>
> **Benefits discussed:** potential to restart epithelial advancement in a stalled
> but otherwise healthy wound, and surface antimicrobial protection without
> systemic antibiotic exposure.
> **Questions:** invited and answered fully. [Patient asked [question]; answered.]
> **Consent: [verbal/written] consent to proceed was obtained from [name] prior to
> application** and is documented here. [[Name] voiced agreement.]

**`.wcmlapply`** — application technique

> Following debridement, the wound bed was **[100]% beefy red granulating tissue**
> with hemostasis confirmed complete. **The [Microlyte] sheet was cut to fit that
> wound bed and placed onto the granulating surface. It adhered to the moisture
> of the wound bed on contact, so no additional saline hydration was required.**
> The product was **positioned to fill the wound bed and to provide a slight
> overhang onto the intact periwound skin, as the manufacturer's IFU advises**, so
> that the margin could be secured. **Steri-Strips were then applied over the
> overhang to hold the product in place.**
>
> Per the IFU, a **non-adherent contact layer (Adaptic) was applied over the
> [Microlyte]** to protect it and to keep the secondary dressing from adhering to
> the wound bed. An **ABD pad was applied over the Adaptic to control drainage**,
> and the area was **wrapped with roll gauze to secure the dressing stack.**
> [Compression was then reapplied over the completed stack at [mmHg].] The patient
> tolerated the application without difficulty[, reporting no increase in pain].
>
> **Dressing stack, in order:** [Microlyte] → Steri-Strips at the margin →
> Adaptic → ABD pad → roll gauze [→ compression].

## Assessment and plan

**`.wcimped`** — impediments with interventions

> **Impediments to healing and what is being done about each:**
> 1. **Unrelieved pressure over the [site]** → q2h turning rewritten and posted;
>    **30° lateral tilt mandated and 90° side-lying prohibited**; no positioning
>    on the [side]; heels floated; HOB ≤ 30°; [Group 2 alternating-pressure
>    mattress and wheelchair cushion ordered]; turn log audit escalated to nursing
>    leadership; caregiver taught with return demonstration.
> 2. **Immobility [from hemiparesis / deconditioning]** → assisted range of motion
>    to both lower extremities [3]× daily; PT/OT [re-]consult; caregiver taught to
>    assist and to prompt on a schedule.
> 3. **[Venous insufficiency with pooling and edema / reduced arterial
>    perfusion]** → sustained [30–40] mmHg compression with **ABI verified before
>    application**; patient instructed not to remove it and told exactly when to
>    call instead; **leg raises [4]× daily and hourly ankle pumps to drive the
>    calf pump**; elevation [3]× daily. [Vascular referral placed.]
> 4. **Protein-calorie malnutrition (albumin [X], [Y]% weight loss)** → [N] g/day
>    protein prescription with named foods, a fortification strategy, supplements
>    between meals, vitamin C, time-limited zinc, dietitian consult, weekly
>    weights.
> 5. **[Infection]** → culture, [antibiotic], imaging, antimicrobial dressing,
>    erythema border marked, written escalation criteria given to nursing.
> 6. **[Hyperglycemia]** → A1c [X]% with an individualized target of < [8.0]%,
>    communicated to [PCP].
> 7. **Adherence and knowledge barriers** → identified specifically ([compression
>    removal, night-shift turning gaps]) and addressed with teach-back and
