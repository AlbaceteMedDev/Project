# Worked Example — Complete Wound Care Progress Note

> **FICTIONAL TEACHING EXAMPLE.** Not a real patient and not a real medical
> record. Names, dates, identifiers, and values are invented to demonstrate the
> documentation standard.

This example is built around a patient who has the problems described in the
brief: a hip pressure ulcer, immobility, poor perfusion with compression, low
albumin, and a falling platelet count. It deliberately shows two wounds behaving
differently on the same visit, because that is where documentation usually breaks
down.

**What to notice while reading:**

- The **trochanteric wound is stalling and infected** — so the advanced matrix is
  **deferred**, and the note says why. Applying a matrix to an infected wound is
  both bad care and a denied claim.
- The **venous leg ulcer is clean and stalled** — it has failed 6 weeks of
  documented compression therapy, so it **meets criteria** and gets the Microlyte
  application with full consent, lot, wastage, and technique.
- **Both wounds enlarged after debridement.** The note says so and explains why,
  in the same paragraph, both times.
- The **platelet count does not carry the antibiotic decision.** The infection
  findings do. The platelets get their own differential and workup.

**Albumin 2.4 g/dL.** Low and declining, consistent with protein-calorie
malnutrition. **Documented interpretation:** albumin is a negative acute-phase
reactant and falls with inflammation independent of intake — with a CRP of
6.8 mg/dL and an actively infected wound, some of this decline is inflammatory.
The malnutrition diagnosis is therefore supported independently by **4.3% weight
loss over 6 weeks (61.0 → 58.4 kg), approximately 50% meal completion by
caregiver and nursing report, and a prealbumin of 12 mg/dL.** Low albumin also
contributes to third-spacing and peripheral edema, which further impairs
perfusion at both wound sites.

**Protein prescription:** 58.4 kg × 1.5 g/kg/day = **88 g protein per day.**
Calories 58.4 × 32 = **1,870 kcal/day.** Renal function permits this loading
(Cr 1.1).
**Current estimated intake:** ~45–50 g/day. **Gap: approximately 40 g/day.**

**How the gap will be closed — specific options given in writing to the patient
and daughter, since "eat more protein" has already failed:**
- **Two high-protein oral supplements daily (20 g each = 40 g)** given *between*
  meals, not with them, so they add to intake rather than displacing it. The
  patient has refused the current shake; **three alternative flavors and a
  clear-liquid protein option were offered and she selected two to trial.**
- **Fortify what she already eats** rather than adding volume — she has early
  satiety. 2 Tbsp dry milk powder into oatmeal, soup, mashed potatoes, and milk
  (+14 g per fortified 8 oz serving); this requires no change in what she is
  willing to eat.
- **High-yield, low-volume foods she reports liking:** Greek yogurt 1 cup (17 g),
  cottage cheese 1 cup (24 g), two eggs (12 g), 3 oz canned tuna (25 g).
- **Arginine/glutamine/HMB supplement, 2 servings daily**, for wound-specific
  support.
- **Vitamin C 500 mg BID.** **Zinc sulfate 220 mg daily (50 mg elemental) —
  STOP DATE 09/11/2026** (14 days; prolonged supplementation induces copper
  deficiency).
- **Registered dietitian consult ordered** for a formal assessment and a
  fortification plan the kitchen can execute. **Weekly weights ordered**, same
  scale, same time of day.
- Repeat albumin, prealbumin, and CRP on 09/11/2026 — interpreted **together**,
  since a falling CRP with a rising prealbumin is the pattern that confirms
  nutritional response rather than resolving inflammation alone.

**Platelets 118 K/µL, down from 147 K/µL on 08/07 (20% decline).** New
thrombocytopenia, mild.

**Differential considered and documented:**
1. **Consumption in the setting of systemic infection.** The patient has clinical
   deep tissue infection (6/7 STONEES), fever, leukocytosis with left shift, and
   a CRP that has more than tripled. In severe infection, platelets fall through
   consumption. *Documented explicitly:* the platelet count is being read as a
   **marker of systemic inflammatory burden and severity**, not as the evidence
   of infection — the infection is established by the wound findings, the fever,
   the leukocytosis, and the CRP/ESR. A falling platelet count in a septic
   patient is a severity signal and warrants closer monitoring.
2. **Medication effect.** Patient is on enoxaparin (VTE prophylaxis) and
   pantoprazole, both associated with thrombocytopenia. **4T score assessed for
   heparin-induced thrombocytopenia: 3 points (low probability)** — the fall is
   20% rather than > 50%, timing is not classic, there is no thrombosis, and
   another cause (sepsis) is present. Enoxaparin continued for now with **daily
   platelet monitoring**; will discontinue and change agents if the count falls
   below 100 K/µL or the 4T score rises.
3. **Nutritional deficiency.** B12 and folate ordered, given documented
   malnutrition and hypoalbuminemia.
4. **Hepatic disease with splenic sequestration** — no history, LFTs ordered.

**Ordered today:** repeat CBC with differential **and peripheral smear** (to
assess for schistocytes and pseudothrombocytopenia), CMP with LFTs, B12, folate,
PT/INR and fibrinogen (to evaluate for DIC given the fall with sepsis), and
**full medication reconciliation with the facility pharmacist.**
**Reassessment:** CBC daily × 3 days, then 09/04/2026. **Escalation:** if
platelets fall below 100 K/µL, if the smear shows schistocytes, or if bleeding
develops → hematology consultation and reconsideration of enoxaparin.

### 5b. Infection management

**Wound #1 meets 6 of 7 STONEES criteria** (size increasing by depth and
undermining, temperature elevated with fever, **probes to bone**, exudate,
erythema and edema, smell), with supporting **leukocytosis 12.8 K/µL with 82%
neutrophils, CRP 6.8 mg/dL, and ESR 68 mm/hr.** This constitutes deep and
surrounding tissue infection, and **systemic antibiotic therapy is indicated on
these findings.** The thrombocytopenia is consistent with this picture and is
being followed as a severity marker; **it is not the basis for the antibiotic
decision.**

- **Tissue culture** obtained from the wound base after cleansing and debridement
  (not a surface swab), sent for aerobic, anaerobic, and fungal culture with
  sensitivities.
- **Empiric therapy started today** after discussion with the SNF attending:
  **ampicillin-sulbactam 3 g IV q6h**, covering the polymicrobial flora typical
  of pressure-injury infection including gram-positives, gram-negatives, and
  anaerobes. Renal dosing appropriate at Cr 1.1. **MRSA coverage was considered**
  and deferred pending culture given no prior MRSA history and no purulent
  cellulitis pattern; will add vancomycin if cultures or clinical course warrant.
  **Antibiotics will be narrowed on sensitivities** — culture follow-up 08/31.
- **Osteomyelitis:** probe-to-bone is positive, which in a Stage 4 pressure
  injury carries a high likelihood of underlying bone involvement.
  **MRI of the right hip and pelvis without contrast ordered**, to be scheduled
  within 72 hours. If osteomyelitis is confirmed, infectious disease consultation
  and a prolonged antibiotic course will be required, and surgical evaluation
  considered.
- **Escalation criteria given to nursing, in writing at the bedside:** T ≥ 101°F,
  new hypotension (SBP < 100), HR > 110, new confusion, spreading erythema beyond
  the marked border, or new drainage → **call this office immediately at [number];
  after hours call [number]. Do not wait for the next scheduled visit.** The
  erythema border was **marked with a skin marker today** so nursing can
  objectively assess spread.
- **Advanced matrix therapy is DEFERRED on wound #1** until the infection is
  controlled and the bed is clean. Applying a wound matrix over an actively
  infected wound with suspected underlying osteomyelitis is contraindicated and
  would not be appropriate care.

## 6. Procedure — debridement

**Two wounds debrided. Documented separately.**

### 6a. Wound #1 — Right greater trochanter

**Indication:** 45% adherent non-viable slough and 10% soft boggy eschar
obstructing the wound bed, in the setting of clinical deep tissue infection.
Debridement is medically necessary to remove the non-viable, infected tissue
burden, disrupt biofilm, obtain a valid tissue culture, and permit accurate
assessment of the wound base and depth.

**Informed consent:** Procedure, purpose, alternatives (enzymatic debridement,
continued dressing therapy alone, no treatment), and risks — pain, bleeding,
infection, need for repeat procedures, and **the likelihood that the measured
wound would appear larger afterward once non-viable tissue was removed** —
discussed with the patient and her daughter, Karen Doe. Questions invited and
answered. **Verbal consent obtained from the patient, who has decision-making
capacity, prior to the procedure.** Site verified; timeout performed.

**Anesthesia:** Topical lidocaine 4% cream, approximately 2 g, applied with a
20-minute dwell time prior to the procedure.

**Technique:** **Selective, non-excisional** sharp debridement — removal of
devitalized tissue only, without excision into viable tissue.
**Instruments:** **4 mm sterile ring curette** for the wound bed; **#15 scalpel
blade** for the fibrotic margin at the 2 to 5 o'clock edge; Adson forceps with
iris scissors for loose slough.
**Tissue removed:** adherent tan-yellow slough, soft boggy eschar, and fibrotic
non-viable tissue at the wound margin.
**Deepest tissue level removed:** devitalized tissue only; **no viable tissue was
excised.** Bone was palpated at the 4 o'clock position but **not debrided** —
deferred pending MRI and infectious disease input.
**Area debrided:** **5.4 sq cm = 35% of the 15.30 sq cm wound surface.**
**Endpoint:** carried to a viable base as evidenced by punctate capillary
bleeding across the debrided surface.

**Bleeding and hemostasis:** **The wound bled during debridement** — punctate
capillary bleeding, expected and desirable as it confirms a viable base. Bleeding
was moderate at the 2 to 5 o'clock fibrotic margin where the #15 blade was used.
**Hemostasis achieved with direct pressure using saline-moistened gauze for
4 minutes**, followed by application of **calcium alginate** to the margin for
its hemostatic property. **Hemostasis was complete and confirmed before any
dressing was applied.** **Estimated blood loss: less than 5 mL.**

**Tolerance:** Patient tolerated the procedure well. **Pain 4/10 during the
procedure** (down from 6/10 at dressing removal, following topical anesthetic).
The procedure was paused twice at her request and resumed after brief rest. No
distress. Vital signs stable throughout; HR remained 92–98.

**Specimen:** **Tissue specimen from the wound base sent for aerobic, anaerobic,
and fungal culture with sensitivities.** No pathology specimen.
**Complications:** None.

**Post-debridement reassessment — wound #1**
**Wound bed now: 85% granulation, 15% adherent slough remaining** at the bone
